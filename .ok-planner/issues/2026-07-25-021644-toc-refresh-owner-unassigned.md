# No skill owns TOC regeneration after sprint deltas

## Problem

The generated header claims the TOCs are 'refreshed whenever a sprint's deltas touch the catalog', but no sprint boilerplate, certification step, or planning step names TOC regeneration; only the compliance reviewer's consistency check would notice staleness after the fact.

## Candidates

- Amend concept:catalog-toc Invariants to assign refresh responsibility to a named ceremony step
- Amend the sprint execution boilerplate (via concept:sprint invariants) to include TOC regeneration in delta application

## Discussion

The question: which skill or ceremony step is responsible for regenerating `concepts.md` / `stories.md` / `decisions.md` after a sprint's corpus deltas change what they should list?

Where it comes from: filed against concept:catalog-toc. Re-verified against current code: the generated header baked into every TOC (visible verbatim atop `.ok-planner/design/concepts.md`, `stories.md`, `decisions.md` in this repo) reads "Generated by `discover-design` and refreshed whenever a sprint's deltas touch the catalog" — an explicit claim of an ongoing refresh responsibility. A search of `plugins/ok-planner/skills/plan-sprint/SKILL.md`, `certify/SKILL.md`, and `audit/SKILL.md` for any mention of `concepts.md`/`stories.md`/`decisions.md`/"TOC"/"table of contents" turns up nothing in any of the three — no step in drafting a sprint, applying its deltas, or certifying completion touches TOC regeneration. The only place TOC content is checked at all is the design-doc compliance reviewer prompt, and that check is read-only consistency verification ("Every TOC bullet's slug matches a live artifact file... Every live artifact file has a TOC entry"), not regeneration — it would flag staleness after the fact (as a finding for `/audit`'s caller, or `/plan-sprint`'s draft reviewer, to fix in-cycle) but nothing proactively regenerates the TOC when a delta lands. The gap the issue reports is unchanged.

What the corpus says: concept:catalog-toc's Purpose calls the TOC "the one-shot-readable form of the corpus" but its Boundaries assign only "discovery of what exists" to the TOC and explicitly hand "TOC consistency... checked by the corpus audit" to a different artifact (corpus-audit, a story) — consistency checking, not generation, is the only responsibility the concept names, and it names a check, not a regenerating actor. Nothing in catalog-toc's Invariants ("Generated content only... Summaries obey self-containment... Entries are alphabetical") assigns an owner or a trigger for regeneration; the header text's "refreshed whenever a sprint's deltas touch the catalog" claim exists only in the generated file itself, not in the concept that is supposed to define the mechanism.

What the code does today: TOC regeneration happens exactly once, during `discover-design`'s bootstrap (step 7, which produces the initial `concepts.md`/`stories.md`/`decisions.md`). No later skill regenerates them; the compliance reviewer's TOC-consistency check is a detector, not a fix, and (per its own scope note) fires only when `audit` runs whole-corpus or when `plan-sprint`'s draft-scope review happens to include TOC-touching deltas.

Candidates as filed: amend concept:catalog-toc's Invariants to assign refresh responsibility to a named ceremony step; amend the sprint execution boilerplate (via concept:sprint's Invariants) to include TOC regeneration in delta application. A third shape worth naming: since concept:sprint's own Boundaries state a sprint "does NOT own execution order" and step 3 of `.ok-planner/CLAUDE.md`'s baked-in execution shape already says "Apply each corpus delta as part of the work that realizes it," TOC regeneration could be folded into that existing step (whoever applies a delta that adds, amends, or retires an artifact also updates that artifact's catalog TOC entry) rather than requiring a new named ceremony step at all — a narrower fix than amending either cited concept.

What the ruling must decide: which point in the sprint-to-corpus pipeline (delta application, `/certify`'s completion contract, or a corpus-audit finding) is responsible for keeping the TOCs current, since none of them currently claims it.
