# Story support is determined from the user's side

## Choice

Story support is determined from the user's vantage: the audit
verifies each story by driving the released product through the
public surface the extraction records — re-running the archived
experiments, repairing
and extending it as claims and surface change — and a story is
`supported` only when a passing surface run demonstrates the
capability and benefit. Decision and concept support keep the
adversarial reading: their claims live behind the surface, where no
user-vantage run can see. Assumptions are verified with the same
surface machinery as stories; the two differ only in where the claim
came from — promised versus presumed.

## Rationale

A story is a user expectation, so the honest test of support is
whether a user can obtain the outcome — and the project's own tests
cannot answer that, because a test may reach behind the surface and
exercise a capability no public element delivers. One instrument for
stories and assumptions also avoids a redundancy: a story verified
twice — once by reading, once by measurement — leaves two verdicts
with two epistemics to reconcile. The vantage line settles vocabulary
for
free — what was verified through the surface can be published in
surface terms with nothing to launder.

## Alternatives

- Reading-based story audits (the prior shape): cheap, but the verdict
  is a developer's inference about a user outcome.
- Test-warranted story audits: the suites already run at every
  release, but a passing test that bypasses the surface proves nothing
  a user can reach.
- User-vantage for everything: decisions and concepts describe
  internals; no surface run can settle them, so their reading audit
  has no substitute.
